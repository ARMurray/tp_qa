## The open question: is a different model worth trying?

Raised at the end of the session, deliberately deferred. It cannot be answered
until there is a valid `12_score_holdout` number, because:

- The ceiling is **91.4%**. No architecture reaches the other 8.6%.
- If rank-1 accuracy sits near 91.4%, recall is binding and a neural net buys
  nothing.
- If it sits well below, there is model headroom and the question is real.

Two further reasons to be careful reading the current metrics as evidence:

1. **Stage 2's reported metrics are measured at 1:50, deployment is ~1:10,535.**
   About 10,500 candidate parcels per plant. The 4.4% false-positive rate behind
   a 0.956 specificity is ~464 false parcels per plant at that density. `04` now
   prints this scaling from the measured specificity.
2. **Permutation importances are all below 0.004** against a 0.99 baseline. No
   single feature is load-bearing, which is what you would expect with
   `log_gisacre`/`ll_gisacre`, `distance_m`/`log_distance` and
   `water_pixels`/`water_pixel_pct` all present. Heavy redundancy, so "add a
   better model" is not obviously where the signal is.

## What changed in the last session

All committed and pushed to `master`. If the HPC has pulled, it has all of this.

### Spatial CV folds were structurally broken — fixed

`spatial_cluster_folds` was `KMeans(n_clusters=5)` with leave-one-cluster-out.
Plants are not uniformly distributed, so k=5 on US plant locations reliably made
one huge cluster and one tiny one — cluster sizes ~59 / 14 / 12 / 10 / 4 % in
three Stage 2 runs a month apart, the same shape each time.

| | Stage 1 before | Stage 2 before | after (both) |
|---|---|---|---|
| smallest test fold | **17 rows** | 645 rows, **10 positives** | ~20% each |
| spatial CV AUC | 0.9490 | 0.9852 | 0.9593 / 0.9895 |
| spatial-vs-standard gap | 0.0144 | 0.0057 | 0.0041 / 0.0014 |

Now clusters into `n_splits * 6` blocks and packs whole blocks into size-balanced
folds, on distinct locations so row counts cannot drag centroids.

**Old spatial CV scores are not comparable across this change.** The
`12_score_holdout` series is unaffected.

The spatial numbers going *up* is the real signal: the old estimate was dragged
down by folds scoring on noise, not by genuine geographic non-transferability.

### Stage 2's reported metrics used to leak — fixed

The final split was `train_test_split` on rows, but each plant contributes ~51
rows sharing every plant-level feature. Measured: **all 395 plants** were on both
sides. Now `GroupShuffleSplit` on `CWNS_ID`.

Worth knowing: I predicted the metrics would drop and **they barely moved**
(roc_auc 0.9911 → 0.9914, sensitivity −1.1pt, specificity −0.8pt). That is
informative — the model was never leaning on the leaked plant-level features.
The importances agree: the top features are all parcel attributes.

Stage 1 needed no such change — `02` dedups `reported_parcels` on `CWNS_ID`, so
it is one row per plant and a row split is already a plant split. It now asserts
that rather than assuming it.

### DuckDB was sized from the node, not the cgroup — fixed

`08` was OOM-killed at 32G in six minutes. `duckdb.connect()` with no
`memory_limit` sizes itself at ~80% of the **node's physical RAM** and knows
nothing about cgroups, so it allocated far past `--mem` and SLURM killed it, with
no DuckDB error at all.

`config.duckdb_connect()` sets `memory_limit` from `SLURM_MEM_PER_NODE` at 70%
and caps `threads` to `SLURM_CPUS_PER_TASK`. All 15 DuckDB users now go through
it. With a limit set, DuckDB spills to disk instead of dying.

Expect two side effects: steps that were oversubscribing threads may get
**faster**, and a step that now spills will be slower but will finish.

### DC dropped from the training universe

One plant, Blue Plains, location already known. `--array=0-50` now, not `0-51`.
The state list existed in **four** places — the copy inside
`02_feature_engineering.slurm` is the one mapping array index to state, so
editing only `_common.sh` would have silently dropped WY instead. `02` and `02b`
now read `DEFAULT_STATES` from `_common.sh`.

### Two crashes fixed, both latent

- **`02` on a state with no matches** — DC's task died with `KeyError: 'CWNS_ID'`.
  A column-less `pd.DataFrame()` where `drop_duplicates` passes silently and the
  merge one line later does not. Stage 2 was already hardened for this; Stage 1
  was not.
- **`02b` had never run.** It passed `args.dry_run` to helpers that take no such
  parameter, and tested `if not merge_file(...)` — always False, since a 2-tuple
  is truthy. Worse, `main()` had no write phase at all: absent the TypeError it
  would have printed "merge complete" having written nothing, leaving the flat
  tables stale for training to consume.

### Stage 1's log was 1,124 warning lines — and they meant something

`SimpleImputer` warns per fit about all-null columns. The columns were
`od_max_conf_drying_bed` (**zero observed values across the entire Stage 1
training set** — that detection class never fired at a single reported location)
and `od_max_conf_chlorine_contact`.

Two of the six classes just added to the models. `report_empty_features()` now
names them once and filters only that exact message.

**Worth following up on the detection side**: the 6-class model carries
`drying_bed` weights but produces nothing at reported locations. Either the class
is genuinely absent there or it is undertrained. `oxidation_pond` had 138 boxes
across 67 tiles; the equivalent count for `drying_bed` is not known.
