## Why an audit slice, and why 80

The move rule's precision so far (46/47 at ≥ 0.95, Wilson 89–100%) comes
from uncertain/random slices, which are not a sample of what the rule moves:
uncertain over-samples small margins and random rarely lands on a move. The
audit slice is a uniform random sample of the plants 13 marked `moved`, so
its precision is the product's precision, with no weighting needed. With 80
audit plants the audit alone gives a 95% Wilson lower bound of 93.3% with one
wrong, 91.3% with two, 89.5% with three. Pooled with rounds 4–5 (127 moves),
up to four wrong keeps the bound above 92%. `calibrate_move_rule.py` already prints results per queue slice, so
the audit shows as its own row.

Audit plants are drawn **before** the uncertain/random slices and keep their
slice on the `KEEP=1` rebuild. They are `candidate_pick` tasks like any
other: the moved-to parcel is the #1 of the five shown, and the reviewer
picks the true parcel as usual.
